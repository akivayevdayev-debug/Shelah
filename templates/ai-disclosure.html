Some things help. The model is told to cite only sources it is sure exist. A check on the finished answer catches explicit content and leaked instructions, though not factual mistakes. Citations link to the source text where Sh'elah can match it, so you can read the original yourself. These reduce the risk and do not remove it. Open the source before you rely on an answer (see {{ _('ai_disclosure.terms_of_service') }} §6).

{{ _('ai_disclosure.6_age_and_sensitive_topics') }}

You must be at least 13 to use Sh'elah, and at least 16 in the EU and UK (see {{ _('ai_disclosure.terms_of_service') }} §3). The AI is told to assume the reader could be 13. On sensitive subjects such as niddah, mikveh and intimacy, it explains the halachic framework in clinical language, cites sources, and sends you to a rabbi, teacher or parent for practical details. If an answer comes back with explicit content anyway, a check replaces it with a short explanation and a referral.
