# Eval: answering every part of a request (2026-10-23)

**The report.** The AI answered part of a request, or refused it with a message that said Sh'elah is only for halachic rulings. A d'var Torah, a eulogy or a list of books was refused as out of scope. Multi-part questions got the short format and lost their later parts. Some answers told the reader to ask a rabbi instead of answering.

## What was wrong

1. **The scope text was too narrow.** The core and simple prompts listed "creative writing" and "personal advice" as out of scope, so the model refused Jewish tasks (a d'var Torah, a eulogy, a seder plan) as if they were rulings it could not give.
2. **The refusal message said the wrong thing.** `_OFF_TOPIC_REFUSAL_TEXT` described Sh'elah as a halachah tool, so even a correct refusal misdescribed the site.
3. **Multi-part requests were classed as simple.** `_is_simple_question` took any question of twelve words or fewer, so "Can I use an electric toothbrush on Shabbat, and what about toothpaste?" got the short format and only one part was answered.
4. **Deferral in place of an answer.** Rule 5 did not forbid "ask your rabbi" as the whole answer, although `rabbinic_disclaimer` already covers the final ruling.

## The fix

- Scope (core, simple, rule 16): the AI helps with anything in Jewish life and learning, not only halachic rulings. Jewish tasks are named (d'var Torah, eulogy, poem, seder/Shabbat/shiva/study plans, books, prayer translation, Jewish recipe, a holiday explained for children, guidance for a non-Jew or convert) with "Do such a task in full, as asked." An everyday task with no Jewish link (a chocolate cake recipe, a joke, a workout plan) is not made Jewish by a kashrut angle and stays out of scope; a Jewish food (challah, cholent, hamantaschen) is in.
- The refusal now reads: "Sh'elah helps with Jewish law, texts, history, customs and everyday Jewish life, and I couldn't find a Jewish angle in {subject}…" (with a matching Hebrew text).
- Rule 5: answer every part (every item, example, step or age group named), and never tell the reader to ask a rabbi in place of answering.
- `_is_multi_part_request` (task verb, a second question, two or more question marks, a list of three, or two commas with ":" or "and") sends the request to the complex format. The complex format says that when the reader asks for something to be produced, the ruling is that thing, written in full. Balanced mode adds the same line.
- `_SCOPE_FIRM_NOTE` keeps the model's judgement when the Jewish word is only incidental (accepted limit 1).
- `PROMPT_VERSION` is `2026-10-23-coverage-v15`.

## Method

`scripts/eval_ai_quality.py coverage` sends 20 Jewish-life requests (tasks, multi-part questions, comparisons) and grades each named part as fully covered, partly covered or missing; a refusal or a "ask your rabbi" deflection is counted separately. `refusal` is the suite from the [2026-10-22 note](2026-10-22-refusals.md). The baseline ran on Claude because Gemini was failing that day, so the change was run on both models.

## Results

| Run | Refused | Deflected | Parts fully covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before (Claude) | 3 (d'var Torah, eulogy, five books) | 2 | 27 / 50 |
| After, Gemini | 0 | 1 | 49 / 62 |
| After, Claude | 0 | 2 | 38 / 57 |

The part count grew because answers that were refused before now list their parts. The one Gemini deflection is the organ-donation donor-card question, where it leaves the personal decision to a rabbi after explaining the views; that is acceptable. The Ashkenazi/Sephardi comparison was moved to the complex format after the Gemini run (`differences?` is a complex signal).

Refusal suite after the change, both models: 0 / 57 false refusals, 13 / 14 correct refusals. The miss is the same as before: "can I use my neighbour's wifi" is answered (as forbidden, theft) rather than refused, which is fine.
